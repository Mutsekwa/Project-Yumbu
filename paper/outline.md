# Paper 1: outline

## Title

**Low-Cost but Not Affordable: Decomposing the Airfare Gap Between Kenya and Mature Low-Cost Markets**

Alternatives:
- *Cheap for Whom? Low-Cost Air Travel and Affordability in Kenya*
- *Low Cost, High Fare: Why Kenya's Low-Cost Airlines Have Not Made Flying Affordable*

## Research question

Why are low-cost airfares in Kenya higher per kilometre than in Europe, North America and Asia, and how far could they fall if Kenya's cost disadvantages were removed?

Sub-questions:
1. **How big is the gap?** Fare per km on Kenyan routes against distance-matched low-cost routes elsewhere.
2. **How big is it relative to income?** Weeks of average income for a return ticket.
3. **What explains it?** Which cost components (fuel, airport and navigation charges, taxes, aircraft size and utilisation, financing) account for the difference.
4. **How far could fares fall?** The fare a Kenyan LCC would need if each disadvantage were brought to European levels, and whether that fare would be affordable on Kenyan incomes.

Sub-question 4 is the heart of the paper. If fares fell to European levels *in dollars* and were still several weeks of income, the conclusion is that cost reform is necessary but not sufficient. That is a finding, not a failure.

## Opening (draft)

> A return flight between Nairobi and Mombasa, little more than an hour in the air, typically costs between US$120 and US$160. On many European low-cost routes of similar length, the same money buys a return ticket to another country. Measured against income, the gap is wider still: a typical return ticket in Africa costs almost seven weeks of national income per person, against less than one week in Europe or North America (IATA, 2019).

## Structure (target 7,000–8,000 words)

1. **Abstract** (200 words, written last)
2. **Introduction**: the hook; why affordability matters (Kenya's travellers, tourism, the Kenyan LCC sector exists and is growing); the research question; your contribution (a transparent, reproducible decomposition of the gap); outline of the paper.
3. **Background and literature**
   - 3.1 The low-cost model and how it lowered fares in Europe, the US and Asia
   - 3.2 Why flying costs more in Africa: fuel, charges, taxes, fleet age, small markets
   - 3.3 Demand and affordability: what drives African air travel, how fares affect it
   - 3.4 The gap your paper fills
4. **Data and method**
   - 4.1 Route sample: Kenyan domestic, East African cross-border, and comparison routes matched by distance
   - 4.2 Fare data: weekly snapshots, booking horizons, nonstop vs connecting, manual validation
   - 4.3 Income data and the "weeks of income" measure
   - 4.4 Cost model (equations, every assumption in a table with its source)
   - 4.5 Counterfactual scenarios
   - 4.6 Limitations
5. **Results**
   - 5.1 The fare gap (fare per km)
   - 5.2 The affordability gap (weeks of income)
   - 5.3 Decomposing the cost gap
   - 5.4 Counterfactual fares
6. **Discussion**: which barriers policy can change (taxes, charges, fuel supply, liberalisation) and which it can't quickly (incomes, market size); compare with published findings; the cross-border premium (the bridge to Paper 2).
7. **Conclusion**: answer the question in one paragraph; future work.
8. **References** (use the target journal's style)
9. **Appendix**: route list, assumption table, link to the GitHub repository.

# Paper 2 (later): intra-African connectivity

**Title idea:** *Open Skies Deferred: Intra-African Connectivity, Fares and the Single African Air Transport Market*

**Question:** Why is travel between African countries so poorly connected and expensive, and is the Single African Air Transport Market (SAATM) changing that?

**Starting facts:** SAATM was launched in 2018 to implement the 1999 Yamoussoukro Decision. AFCAC reports 38 states have joined, 26 have signed Memoranda of Implementation and 21 take part in the pilot implementation programme (check the latest figures when you start).

**Method:** route network built from airport destination tables; count city pairs with direct service versus routings through hubs inside and outside Africa; compare fares and connectivity for SAATM implementers against non-implementers over time.
